# TREATMENT — What Happens to People Who Never Heard of Jesus? (Stained Glass, ~3 min)

Style: `styles/stained-glass/STYLE.md` (Stained Glass). Language: English (user's language).
Format: 1920×1080, 24 fps, ~3 min (narration-led). Voice: Kokoro offline storyteller. No LemoLab credit (user's film).

## 1. Three candidate structures

- **A. Three windows, three answers** (chosen): one great window of three lancets; the sun wakes each lancet in turn as the narration gives Answer 1 (Cornelius), Answer 2 (light in every heart), Answer 3 (the fair Judge). The window is the argument: three panes, one light.
- **B. Rose window as a dial**: petals lit like hours, centre the answer. Beautiful but abstract; the script is narrative (a man, a house, a city), not a cycle; cut.
- **C. One tall lancet bottom-to-top**: a single life read upward. The script has three distinct scenes (Caesarea, Athens, Sodom); one lancet would cramp them; cut.

Why A: the script is literally three answers; the style's native move (light wakes one pane at a time) *is* the structure. The light theme ("little light", "no one born without light") is the sun moving across the window.

## 2. Logline and arc

A question from the comments — what happens to people who never heard of Jesus? — answered in three windows of glass: God sends the gospel to the man reaching for Him (Cornelius), no one is born without light (creation, conscience, Athens), and God judges every person fairly by what they were given (Sodom). Setup (the question) → turn (three answers) → ending (all views agree: through Jesus alone; Paul's question puts the weight back on us).

## 5. Beat sheet (narration lines → windows)

1. HOOK (dark window, first light): "What happens to people who never heard of Jesus? If Jesus is the only way to the Father, what about a man born in a remote village who died without ever hearing his name?"
2. CORNELIUS (lancet 1 wakes): "People ask this in our comments all the time, so let's allow the Bible to answer it. In Caesarea, there lived a Roman officer named Cornelius, a Gentile who had never heard the gospel preached. Yet the Bible says he feared God with all his house, gave much to the poor, and prayed to God continually."
3. ANGEL + PETER (lancet 1 holds): "One afternoon, an angel told him to send for a man called Peter, who was lodging by the sea in Joppa. When Peter walked into that house, he said, 'God is no respecter of persons, but in every nation he that feareth him is accepted with him.'"
4. ANSWER 1 (lancet 1 full light): "So the first answer is this. God saw a man reaching for him with the little light he had, and he sent the gospel straight to his door."
5. ANSWER 2 (lancet 2 wakes): "The second answer is that no one is born without any light at all. Paul writes that God's eternal power is clearly seen in creation, and that his law is written on the hearts of people who never had the scriptures."
6. ATHENS (lancet 2 detail): "When Paul walked through Athens, he found an altar carved with the words, 'To the unknown God,' built by people reaching for a God they couldn't name."
7. ANSWER 3 (lancet 3 wakes, dusk): "The third answer is that God will judge every person fairly by what they were given. Jesus said, 'Unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required.'"
8. SODOM (lancet 3 holds): "Centuries earlier, Abraham pleaded with God for the city of Sodom and asked, 'Shall not the judge of all the earth do right?' God agreed to spare the whole city if even 10 righteous people could be found inside it."
9. AGREEMENT (whole window): "Christians still disagree on exactly how God deals with those who never heard. Some believe a person must hear of Jesus and trust him in this life, while others believe Christ can save people who never learned his name. But every one of those views agrees that no one is saved except through Jesus."
10. WEIGHT (floor patch): "Then Paul asks a question that puts the weight back on our shoulders." (script ends here — closing hook for part 2)

## 6. Cue map

Free unmetred chant for darkness (hook); organ pedal drone under Cornelius; harp arpeggio on each pane waking (one note = "the light"); bells on "God is no respecter of persons"; dusk = deep orange, organ holds then cuts out at Sodom; full window = organ + bells together; floor patch = single long-reverb bell. Every pane waking, crack and bell lands on a listed cue.

## 8. Type

Subtitles: parchment banderole near the bottom (curled ends, gentle sag), dark ink, unfurls from centre; text fades in at ≥75% open; hold ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Titles: incised Roman capitals in stone, revealed by moving light band. In-glass text only as grisaille on white-glass scrolls (altar inscription, Peter's words). `.srt` exported from the same subtitle array (`subtitles_en.srt`).

STYLE §1 says the craft is secular (no crosses/saints/halos). The user's topic is explicitly the Bible and Jesus, so the film tells *their* story in the craft: scenes from the script (officer, angel as light-figure, altar, city) drawn as glass pieces. No decorative iconography beyond the story's needs.
